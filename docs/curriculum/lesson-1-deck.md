## You just did what it does

<div class="quote">

You picked the word that usually comes next. When you'd seen the pattern a lot, you were
right. When you hadn't, you still answered. Some of you sounded sure.

</div>

That's a language model.

## What a language model is

A language model read an enormous amount of text and learned which words tend to **follow**
which.

When you ask it something, it writes an answer one piece at a time, each time picking a
**likely** next piece.

<!--
8 minutes for this slide and the next two. High school: ask what it could not have read, and
how that shapes what it says.
-->

---

## Two things it is not

<div class="cards two">
<div class="card-panel">
<h3>Not a search engine</h3>
<p>It does not <strong>look things up</strong> unless something hands it sources. On its own,
it remembers patterns.</p>
</div>
<div class="card-panel">
<h3>Not a person who knows</h3>
<p>It does not know when it is wrong. It sounds the same when it's right and when it's
<strong>guessing</strong>.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="box">

A confident wrong answer is called a **hallucination**.

</div>

---

## If it sounds sure either way, how could you tell?

<div class="quote">

You check it against something **real**.

</div>

## Every answer has two parts

<div class="cards two">
<div class="card-panel">
<h3>1. The overview</h3>
<p>The model's <strong>own words</strong>, from what it already knows. It can be wrong and has
no Citations.</p>
</div>
<div class="card-panel">
<h3>2. Books in our library</h3>
<p><strong>Real books</strong> from our Catalog. Each has a <strong>Citation</strong>: title and
call number.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="quote">

This can be wrong, check the source.

</div>

## Your Card

- It lets your group use the assistant **for this period**.
- It has a number on it. The assistant **never learns your name**.
- The librarian can see the questions, with the Card number.
- Ask about books and topics. **Don't type** your full name, address, or phone number, or
  anyone else's.
