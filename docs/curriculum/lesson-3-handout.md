## Four steps

| Step | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **Question** | What does the answer claim? What might be missing? |
| **Check** | What evidence supports it? Which claims need checking? |
| **Compare** | Check it against reliable sources: the Record, then the book. |
| **Decide** | What is trustworthy, what needs fixing, what shouldn't be used? |

## What it's good and bad at

- You can ask about almost any subject, math included. The overview is often a useful start.
- It can be wrong about any of it, multi-step math especially. Check the steps yourself or in a
  book.
- It's strongest at finding books in our collection, because that comes straight from our
  Catalog.
- "The library's catalog has nothing on this yet" is an honest answer. The overview is then
  unchecked.

## Take it with you

- **Real books don't make the overview right.** It was written before the books were found.
- Check the **claim**, not just that books were listed.
- Use the steps: **Question, Check, Compare, Decide**.
- Being wrong about one thing doesn't make everything wrong. Show your evidence.

## Check yourself

1. Why can an overview be wrong when every book under it is real?
2. What are the three verdicts, and what does each one need?
3. What was the hardest kind of mistake to catch? Why?
