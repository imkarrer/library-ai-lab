## Three AI Use Tiers

| Tier | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Human-Only** | No AI tools for this work. Your own thinking, your own words. | An in-class quiz; a personal reflection |
| **AI-Assist** | You may use AI for a step the teacher names. Check what it gave you, and disclose it. | Using the Library Assistant to find books for a research project |
| **AI-Optional** | You may use AI more freely. You still disclose it and check it. You're responsible for everything you turn in. | A practice activity on how AI answers compare to sources |

## My disclosure line

<div class="quote">

**AI use:** I used <span class="blank"></span> (tool) to <span class="blank"></span> (what for)
on <span class="blank"></span> (date). I kept <span class="blank"></span> and changed

</div>

**Partner check.** Does the line say:

- ☐ what tool?
- ☐ what for?
- ☐ what was kept or changed?
- ☐ how it was checked?

## An example

*AI use: I used the Library Assistant to find books about volcanoes on March 3. I kept two of the
books it suggested and skipped one that was about earthquakes. I checked it by finding both books
on the shelf and reading their tables of contents.*

When the assignment is Human-Only, the line is: *AI use: none.*

## Take it with you

- **The tier belongs to the assignment**, not to you. Check it each time.
- **Disclosing isn't confessing.** It's what good researchers do, the same as citing a book.
- The tiers and the line apply to **every AI tool**, including ones you use at home.
- **You're responsible** for what you turn in.
